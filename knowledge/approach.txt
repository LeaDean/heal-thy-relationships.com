APPROACH & METHODS
==================

At the heart of our work is a simple but profound observation: most of our suffering in relationships doesn't come from other people — it comes from the patterns, stories, and automatic reactions we bring to them. Change the pattern, and everything changes.

We work with people to help them see these patterns clearly, understand where they come from, and — most importantly — find practical ways to respond differently. The focus is always practical: small, repeatable steps you can weave into your normal routine without needing special time, tools, or retreats.

NOTICING BEFORE REACTING
-------------------------
One of the most powerful things a person can develop is the ability to catch themselves in the moment before a habitual reaction kicks in. Not to suppress the reaction, but simply to create a small space between the trigger and the response. In that space, choice becomes possible.

- Acknowledge the emotion the moment it shows up. Pause for one conscious breath and name it silently: "This is anger rising" or "Fear is here." This prevents the slow buildup that turns small feelings into big explosions later.
- Track your own patterns in real time: notice automatic reactions without judgment. The simple act of noticing creates space between you and the habit.
- Intentionally break small daily habits: do one routine thing differently each day. This keeps you awake and prevents life from running on autopilot.

WORKING WITH EMOTIONS
---------------------
Emotions are natural signals and sources of energy — never the enemy. The key is to meet them cleanly instead of pushing them away or letting them run the show.

- Use the emotion as fuel instead of letting it use you. Channel anger into something useful — speak more clearly, finish a task, or move your body. Let fear's alertness sharpen your attention.
- Ride the wave instead of resisting the peak. Soften your body and let the sensation move through like a wave. Full allowance shortens emotional storms from hours to minutes.
- Separate the story from the feeling. Name the feeling first, then gently set the story aside. Ask: "What does this feeling actually need right now?"
- Transform recurring emotional triggers with micro-actions. Keep a short list of your top three repeating triggers. Each time one appears, do one tiny opposite action.

UNDERSTANDING YOUR PATTERNS
----------------------------
We all have patterns — ways of relating, defending, withdrawing, or pushing forward that were once useful but may no longer serve us. These patterns often feel like "just how I am," but they are learned responses, and what was learned can be understood and, where helpful, changed.

Many relationship difficulties are maintained by habitual cycles — the same argument that never resolves, the same withdrawal, the same words said in the same tone with the same result. We work with people to identify these cycles and find the specific points where a different choice is possible.

THE STORIES WE TELL OURSELVES
------------------------------
We interpret events constantly, building narratives about ourselves, others, and the world. "He always does this." "She doesn't care." "I'm not good enough." These stories feel like facts, but they are interpretations — and they powerfully shape our experience and behaviour.

Part of our work is helping people examine these stories with curiosity rather than accepting them as fixed truths. Separate the story from the feeling. Ask: what actually happened, and what meaning did I add to it?

RELATIONSHIPS AS MIRRORS
-------------------------
The people we are closest to often reflect back to us the things we find hardest to see in ourselves. When something in a relationship triggers a strong reaction, that very reaction is worth exploring — it often points directly to something important within ourselves.

- View difficult conversations or relationships as mirrors. Ask: "What part of me is this showing me?"
- Stay present under pressure — return to breath and physical sensations.
- Listen from silence — empty your mind of your own agenda while the other person speaks.
- Use silence as a response when emotions spike.

ENERGY MANAGEMENT
-----------------
- Scan and reclaim your energy multiple times daily. Every couple of hours, notice where your attention has leaked and breathe it back consciously.
- End unfinished cycles before sleep. Review the day and mentally complete any open loops.
- Evening or morning life review with breath: 5-10 minutes mentally walking through recent interactions, breathing in scattered energy and breathing out leftover tension.

WORKING WITH INTENT
-------------------
- Set a clear daily intention each morning: 30 seconds to state how you want to meet whatever comes.
- Make tiny, conscious agreements with yourself instead of big resolutions.
- Choose your battles with precision — ask "Is this worth my life energy?"
- Give without expectation or bookkeeping.

TAKING RESPONSIBILITY WITHOUT SELF-BLAME
-----------------------------------------
There is an important distinction between taking responsibility for our part in a situation and blaming ourselves for everything. Taking responsibility is empowering — it means we have agency. Self-blame is simply another way of staying stuck.

These aren't one-time fixes; they're lived skills that become more natural the more you use them. In one-to-one sessions these are customised to your specific life — your relationships, work stress, health concerns, or whatever feels stuck.
